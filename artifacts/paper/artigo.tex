\begin{abstract}
  \textbf{Research Context.} Project observatories observe a set of projects and
  disseminate information about them to sustain transparency and accountability.
  The Model for Project Observatories (MPO), the reference conceptual model of
  this domain, is described in natural language and has been formalized in
  OntoUML and OWL by published work.
  \textbf{Scientific and/or Practical Problem.} Natural-language reference
  models underdetermine their own formalization. Two initiatives may declare
  adherence to MPO while adopting semantically incompatible interpretations, so
  adherence is unverifiable and comparison between observatories rests on
  terminological coincidence.
  \textbf{Proposed Solution and/or Analysis.} MPO is submitted to an ontological
  analysis grounded in the Unified Foundational Ontology and conducted through
  its published formalization: each deficiency is located in that artifact and
  traced to where MPO leaves the interpretation open. The revised model is the
  contribution, not the formalization of MPO.
  \textbf{Related IS Theory.} Representation Theory (Wand \& Weber) supplies the
  typology that classifies every finding: construct overload, redundancy, excess
  and deficit.
  \textbf{Research Method.} Design Science Research frames the work, continuing
  the cycle that produced MPO; Methontology guides construction. The published
  model is reconstructed as a baseline, revised, transformed into OWL through
  gUFO, instantiated over an observatory scenario and interrogated by seven
  domain competency questions in SPARQL, verified before and after revision.
  \textbf{Summary of Results.} Nine representational deficiencies were exposed
  in the published formalization, spanning construct overload, invalid
  mereology, semantic collapse and missing event and agent microtheories, each
  classified, traced to MPO and corrected. Every competency question returned
  correct answers, and verification reports improved between runs.
  \textbf{Contributions and Impact to IS area.} The work delivers a verifiable
  basis for auditing adherence to MPO, provenance traceability for
  accountability, and a reusable procedure for auditing natural-language
  reference models, addressing semantic interoperability among information
  ecosystems, a named challenge of the Brazilian IS agenda.
  \textbf{Keywords.} Project Observatory; Ontological Analysis; Representation
  Theory; Conceptual Modeling; Design Science Research.
\end{abstract}

\begin{resumo}
  \textbf{Contexto da pesquisa.} Observatórios de projetos observam um conjunto
  de projetos e divulgam informação sobre eles para sustentar transparência e
  prestação de contas, e seu modelo de referência, o Modelo para Observatórios
  de Projetos (MPO), é descrito em linguagem natural.
  \textbf{Problema.} Modelos de referência em prosa subdeterminam sua própria
  formalização: duas iniciativas podem se declarar aderentes ao MPO com
  interpretações semanticamente incompatíveis.
  \textbf{Solução e análise.} O MPO é submetido a uma análise ontológica
  fundamentada na UFO, conduzida através de sua formalização publicada; o modelo
  revisado é a contribuição.
  \textbf{Teoria de SI.} A Representation Theory (Wand \& Weber) fornece a
  tipologia que classifica cada achado.
  \textbf{Método.} Design Science Research como envelope e Methontology na
  construção, com transformação para OWL pela gUFO, instanciação e sete questões
  de competência em SPARQL.
  \textbf{Resultados.} Nove deficiências representacionais expostas,
  classificadas, rastreadas até o MPO e corrigidas.
  \textbf{Contribuições para SI.} Base verificável para auditar aderência,
  rastreabilidade de proveniência e um procedimento reusável para auditar
  modelos de referência em linguagem natural.
  \textbf{Palavras-chave.} Observatório de Projetos; Análise Ontológica;
  Representation Theory; Modelagem Conceitual; Design Science Research.
\end{resumo}
